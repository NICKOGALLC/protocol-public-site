Protocol Personal Health

Privacy policy

Effective October 21, 2026

Protocol is a private, self-hosted personal health tracker. The installation owner controls its records, connected accounts, devices, and backups.

1. What this policy covers

This policy describes Protocol Personal Health, its Google Health connector, its optional paired Android controls, and this public information site. Protocol is operated for the installation owner’s personal use; this public site is not a hosted health-record service.

2. Google Health access and use

Google Health access requires the account owner’s explicit authorization. Protocol requests three read-only permission categories: activity and fitness; health metrics and measurements; and sleep.

Depending on what Google makes available for the authorized account, records can include activity totals and exercise sessions; heart rate, resting heart rate, heart-rate variability and heart-rate zones; oxygen saturation, breathing rate and sleep-temperature measurements; and sleep sessions and summaries. Availability and source attribution vary. Protocol does not assume every measurement comes from one particular wearable.

Protocol uses this information to provide the owner with personal health records, daily summaries, trend charts, heart-rate curves, sleep and exercise views, and comparisons with recorded doses, measurements, lab results, and optional context. Comparisons are descriptive and do not establish causation. The connection does not write, change, or delete records in Google Health.

Protocol Personal Health’s use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Google user data is not sold, used for advertising, or used to develop or train generalized artificial intelligence or machine-learning models.

3. Other records and Android controls

The owner may enter protocols, dose history, measurements, inventory, journal entries, personal context, and workout sessions, or upload lab reports and other attachments. These records are stored in the private Protocol installation. Lab PDF extraction runs within that installation rather than uploading reports to this public site.

The optional Android companion can read a wearable’s shared Bluetooth heart rate and capture personal workout sessions. It stores its pairing credential and pending session data on the phone and sends authorized readings or sessions to the owner’s Protocol server. Google OAuth credentials are not sent to the phone. These controls do not remotely activate the wearable’s internal workout mode.

4. Storage, retention, and backups

Health records are stored on the owner’s self-hosted systems in Protocol’s database and attachment storage. Google connection credentials are kept separately in private files outside the source repository. The owner controls access to those systems.

Long-term wearable storage retains daily metric summaries, 15-minute heart-rate curves, and compact sleep and exercise sessions. Full-resolution provider payloads are temporary import data and are discarded after successful conversion; abandoned temporary data is cleared during collection cleanup. Daily summaries are retained until the owner removes them. Other authored records and attachments remain until removed by the owner.

Owner-created backups may contain retained health summaries and other personal records. Protocol’s application backup archives exclude Google OAuth credentials, Android pairing credentials, live heart-rate readouts, and temporary import data. Separately managed system backups may have different coverage. Deleting live records does not automatically remove existing backup copies; the owner manages those copies separately.

5. Access and sharing

Health records are used for the owner-facing features described above. Protocol does not automatically publish them, provide them to advertising networks, sell them, or send them to this public website or its repository.

The owner may explicitly authorize access or sharing for a specific purpose, including technical support or development. Human access to Google user data is limited to the owner and people the owner explicitly authorizes for permitted purposes, or where required for security or by law. Any authorized use or transfer remains subject to the Google API Services User Data Policy and Limited Use requirements.

Google processes the original wearable data and account authorization under its own policies. The public site’s hosting provider, GitHub, processes website requests separately, as described below.

6. Your controls and deletion

The owner can disable Automatic Sync and pause the historical import in Protocol’s Health → Connection panel. Google access can be revoked through Google Account connections. Paired Android controls can be revoked in Protocol.

Revoking Google access stops future authorized collection; it does not erase records already imported into Protocol. The installation owner controls local record removal and backups. Removing connector credentials, local health data, and retained backup copies are separate administrative actions. Reauthorization is needed before Google collection can resume after access is revoked or expires.

For a privacy or removal request involving this installation, contact its owner through the contact route below. Do not include health records, credentials, or other sensitive information in a public issue.

7. This public website

This information site contains static pages. It has no forms, sign-in, analytics scripts, embedded health charts, or access to Protocol’s private database. It does not set application cookies or store visitor information in browser storage.

GitHub Pages hosts the public site. GitHub states that it logs and stores visitors’ IP addresses for security purposes. See the GitHub Pages data-collection notice and GitHub Privacy Statement. Following an external link subjects that visit to the destination’s own policies.

8. Contact and policy changes

For site or privacy questions, contact the operator through the Protocol public-site repository. Send only a non-sensitive request to arrange private communication. Users of a separate private installation should contact their own installation owner.

This policy will be updated when the application’s data practices change. The effective date above identifies the current version.
